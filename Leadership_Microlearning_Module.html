Burns (1978) · Bass & Avolio (1994)
The Core Distinction

James MacGregor Burns first drew the distinction in 1978. Transactional leadership is an exchange — performance for reward. Transformational leadership elevates both leader and follower beyond self-interest toward a shared purpose. Both are legitimate. The question is when to use each.

Bass & Avolio's Four I's of Transformational Leadership

Idealised Influence — The leader is a role model; followers trust and identify with them.

Inspirational Motivation — The leader articulates a compelling vision that gives work meaning.

Intellectual Stimulation — The leader challenges assumptions and encourages creative problem-solving.

Individualised Consideration — The leader treats each person as unique, coaching and mentoring them individually.

"The transforming leader looks for potential motives in followers, seeks to satisfy higher needs, and engages the full person of the follower."

— James MacGregor Burns, Leadership (1978)

The Augmentation Effect

Bass (1985) showed that Transformational leadership augments Transactional leadership — it adds value on top. The most effective academic leaders use Transactional methods as a foundation (clear expectations, fair rewards) and layer Transformational behaviours (vision, inspiration, coaching) on top.

In Academic Leadership — What Research Shows

Studies in higher education consistently show that Transformational leadership is positively associated with faculty research output, job satisfaction, and organisational commitment. However, Transactional leadership remains essential for quality assurance, compliance, and process management — areas where academic institutions cannot afford ambiguity.

Reading the Situation
The skilled leader asks: what does this moment need?
The Diagnostic Question

Before choosing your leadership approach, ask: "Does this situation need more clarity and accountability — or more vision and inspiration?" The answer determines your style.

🔌
Use Transactional When...

● Tasks are routine and processes are established
● Quality and compliance standards must be maintained
● The team is experienced and performing well
● Accountability gaps need to be closed quickly
● Crisis requires immediate, directive action

🌟
Use Transformational When...

● The department needs a new direction or cultural shift
● Faculty are disengaged or lacking purpose
● You are building a research culture from scratch
● Talented people need to be challenged and developed
● You are navigating significant institutional change

⚖
The 3-Question Leader's Check

Before your next significant leadership action, pause and ask:

1. What does my team need most right now — clarity or inspiration?

2. Am I choosing this style intentionally or out of habit?

3. What would change if I shifted my approach today?
